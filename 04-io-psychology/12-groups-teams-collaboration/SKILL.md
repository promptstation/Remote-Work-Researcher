# Groups, Teams, and Collaboration

## Mission

A team is interdependent. Diagnose Inputs–Processes–Outputs: who is on it, how they work, what the org provides, and what "good" is at the team level. Individual star ratings will miss the object.

## Core output

A **Team Diagnosis**: interdependence type, composition, processes, context, team-level criterion, intervention.

## Phase 1 — Is it a team?

If work is pooled with little interdependence, "team building" is a cost. If reciprocal/intensive, process and safety matter.

## Phase 2 — IPO

**Inputs:** size, skills, diversity, stability, time allocation.  
**Processes:** coordination, goal sharing, conflict (task vs relationship), backup behaviour, psychological safety (speaking up about errors — not "being nice").  
**Context:** rewards (individual vs team — personnel economics), information tools, leadership, virtuality.

Virtual teams: more need for explicit coordination and documentation; not a different species.

## Phase 3 — Measurement

Team performance, not mean of individual ratings. Process surveys need aggregation justification (rwg, ICC). Safety climate/psychological safety are group properties.

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Interdependence.
2. Criterion.
3. Composition vs process vs context.
4. Intervention at that layer (staffing, process norms, reward mix, tools).
5. Evaluate at team level.

## Anti-patterns

- Offsites for a staffing problem
- Psychological safety as no disagreement
- Individual bonuses only on interdependent work
- Average of 360s as team performance
- Size of 15+ as a "team"

## Validation gate

Interdependence named; team-level criterion; layer of diagnosis named.

## Final principle

Teams fail at a layer. Treat the layer, not the myth of bonding.
