# Leadership and Management

## Mission

Leadership is influence toward collective goals. Evidence is stronger for specific behaviours (task structure, consideration, high-quality LMX, contingent reward) than for heroic personality myths. Fashionable styles are hypotheses.

## Core output

A **Leadership Diagnosis**: level (person, dyad, team, system), behaviours vs traits, outcomes, intervention that can be evaluated.

## Phase 1 — Levels

A "leadership problem" may be selection of managers, skill, dyadic relationships, team process, or structure (too many directs, no authority). Do not send everyone to a retreat until the level is named.

## Phase 2 — Evidence-based levers

- **Task and relationship behaviours:** both; contingency on the work
- **LMX:** quality of dyadic exchange; watch in-groups
- **Transactional / contingent reward:** often under-sold, works
- **Transformational behaviours:** vision, intellectual stimulation — real but over-packaged; common-method bias in many studies
- **Traits:** some GMA and extraversion/conscientiousness prediction of emergence; weak license to hire only "charisma"

Dark side: abusive supervision, narcissism — measure outcomes (turnover, safety, silence), not only engagement with the leader.

## Phase 3 — Development

Behavioural practice, feedback, and context beat a personality workshop. If the org rewards firefighting, training "coaching" will not stick (transfer).

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Outcome (team performance, safety, retention).
2. Level.
3. Behaviours to change.
4. Intervention + evaluation (not a happy-sheet of the offsite).

## Anti-patterns

- One true style
- Guru quadrants
- Ignoring structure
- 360 as a popularity contest
- Charisma as a selection bar without job analysis

## Validation gate

Level named; behaviours not only traits; outcome named; fashion models not used as evidence.

## Final principle

Leaders act in systems. If you cannot name the behaviour and the outcome, you are buying a story about leaders, not I-O.
