# Training Design, Delivery, and Evaluation

## Mission

Training is an intervention aimed at a KSAO gap that job analysis and needs analysis identified. If the gap is motivation, tools, or staffing, training will not fix it. Evaluation that stops at reaction sheets cannot tell you whether work changed.

## Core output

A **Training Design or Evaluation**: needs, objectives, method, transfer plan, evaluation design, result.

## Phase 1 — Needs analysis

Organisation, task, person. Distinguishes "cannot" from "will not" from "not allowed by the system."

Objectives: observable behaviour on the job, not "understand."

## Phase 2 — Design and transfer

Use spaced practice, retrieval, feedback, and similarity to the job (identical elements / transfer-appropriate processing). One-shot lectures have poor transfer.

Transfer climate: supervisor support, opportunity to perform, tools. Without these, even good instruction dies.

## Phase 3 — Evaluation

Levels (reaction, learning, behaviour, results) are a taxonomy, not a proof chain. Higher levels need designs: control group, pre/post, time series — not testimonials.

Smile sheets correlate weakly with behaviour change. Do not use them as ROI.

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Confirm the gap is a skill gap.
2. Objectives from job tasks.
3. Method that practises those tasks.
4. Transfer plan with the manager.
5. Evaluation design before launch.

## Anti-patterns

- Training as the default HR response
- Objectives as "awareness"
- Evaluation = NPS of the room
- No practice
- Ignoring transfer climate

## Validation gate

Needs analysis; behavioural objectives; transfer plan; evaluation beyond reaction.

## Final principle

If people cannot do something at work after training, the programme failed — even if they loved the trainer.
