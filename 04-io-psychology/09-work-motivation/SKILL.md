# Work Motivation

## Mission

Motivation is the direction, intensity, and persistence of work effort. Diagnose which theory's conditions are broken (expectancy, goals, equity, needs/SDT, job characteristics) before installing a bonus or a poster.

## Core output

A **Motivation Diagnosis**: observed behaviour, broken link (expectancy/instrumentality/valence, goal, justice, autonomy/competence/relatedness, job characteristics), lever, what not to do.

## Phase 1 — Theories as checklists

- **Expectancy (VIE):** effort→performance believed? performance→outcomes believed? outcomes valued?
- **Goal-setting:** specific, difficult, accepted, feedback, resources
- **Equity / justice:** inputs/outcomes vs referents; procedural voice
- **Job characteristics:** variety, identity, significance, autonomy, feedback
- **SDT:** autonomy, competence, relatedness — crowding-out if pay is controlling

Pick the one that matches the symptoms. Multiple can be true; sequence the cheapest test.

## Phase 2 — Do not skip the job

If tools, staffing, or unclear goals are the problem, "motivation" is a mislabel. If incentives pay for the wrong metric, personnel-economics gaming is the problem.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. What behaviour is missing (direction vs intensity vs persistence)?
2. Walk VIE and goals first (most operational).
3. Check justice and job design.
4. Choose one lever; evaluate.

## Anti-patterns

- Speeches as intervention
- More money when instrumentality is broken (they do not believe pay is tied to performance)
- Goals without resources
- Ignoring inequity
- Treating all work as intrinsically motivating

## Validation gate

Theory named; broken link named; lever matches; job constraints considered.

## Final principle

People usually have reasons. Motivation theory is how you find which reason, instead of shouting louder.
