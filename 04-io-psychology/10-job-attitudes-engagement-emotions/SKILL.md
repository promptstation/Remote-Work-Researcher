# Job Attitudes, Engagement, and Emotions

## Mission

Attitudes are evaluations of the job or organisation. They predict some behaviours (turnover intent, citizenship) modestly and performance weakly-to-moderately. An engagement score is not output. Treat it as an attitude measure with antecedents and consequences.

## Core output

An **Attitude Brief**: construct, items, psychometric notes, levels and gaps, likely antecedents, what the score may and may not be used for.

## Phase 1 — Constructs

- **Job satisfaction:** evaluative; facets (pay, supervisor, work itself) often more useful than global
- **Commitment:** affective, continuance, normative — different behaviours
- **Engagement:** vigorous involvement; overlap with satisfaction and with extraversion/affect; vendor definitions vary — demand the items
- **Affect/emotions:** state vs trait; daily events

Do not mix them in one "culture score."

## Phase 2 — Measurement

Reliable scales, anonymity, response rate and nonresponse. Item-level drivers from multiple regression on the same survey are not causes (common method; see DS claim-sort skill).

Benchmark vendor percentiles are marketing unless the norm group is named.

## Phase 3 — Links

Satisfaction–performance: real but modest; more for citizenship than core task in many studies. Satisfaction–turnover: more reliable.

If the business outcome is profit, you still need an operational path (service, quality, retention), not a leap from eNPS to EBITDA.

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Name the construct and show items.
2. Psychometrics and response bias.
3. Facet and group splits.
4. Hypothesise antecedents from job design, leadership, justice — not from "the score is low so we need pizza."
5. Tie to a behaviour you will also measure.

## Anti-patterns

- Engagement as performance
- Driver analysis as causality
- Secret surveys
- One global number, no facets
- Action planning as posters

## Validation gate

Construct = items; not treated as output; facets/groups shown; actions aimed at antecedents.

## Final principle

Attitudes matter and they are not the work. Measure them as attitudes, then follow the behaviour.
