# Occupational Health, Stress, and Well-Being

## Mission

Strain follows demands minus resources, plus hazards. Individual resilience training that leaves workload, control, and safety climate untouched is a misdirection. I-O's first lever is the job and the organisation.

## Core output

A **Work-and-Health Note**: stressors (task, role, social, physical), resources, outcomes (strain, burnout dimensions, safety events), intervention level (primary/secondary/tertiary).

## Phase 1 — Map

Job demands–resources / demand–control–support: high demands + low control + low support is the classic risk pattern. Role ambiguity and conflict, injustice, harassment, night work, and physical hazards are stressors — not "mindset."

Burnout: exhaustion, cynicism, professional efficacy — not a synonym for tired.

Safety: climate and leading indicators, not only lagging accident counts.

## Phase 2 — Intervention levels

- **Primary:** change the job (load, control, staffing, schedule, hazards)
- **Secondary:** skills to cope with remaining demands
- **Tertiary:** support for people already harmed (EAP, clinical referral)

Do not sell secondary as primary. Wellness perks are not job redesign.

If someone is in clinical distress, this skill does not treat them; point to appropriate care and to organisational causes in parallel.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Outcomes (absence, incidents, burnout items, turnover).
2. Demand/control/support and hazards.
3. Primary changes first.
4. Evaluate with the same outcomes, not app-usage stats.

## Anti-patterns

- Yoga for understaffing
- Burnout as individual weakness
- Safety as posters
- Ignoring harassment and injustice
- Wellness ROI theatre

## Validation gate

Job stressors named; primary vs secondary distinguished; clinical cases not "I-O'd away."

## Final principle

Well-being at work is mostly designed. If the job is the pathogen, a workshop is not the medicine.
