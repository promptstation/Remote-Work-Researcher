# Employment Intelligence Process and Decision Questions

## Mission

Intelligence is a decision support product, not a data dump. Name the decision, the market (occupation × industry × geography × contract type), the horizon, and the confidence the user needs. Then collect. Never start with a 40-tab workbook.

## Core output

An **Intelligence Requirement**: decision, market definition, questions, sources, product type (spot brief vs monitor), confidence bar, ethics.

## Phase 1 — Decision

Examples: hire where, pay how much, which skill to train, whether a shortage is real, where to site a team, whether to freeze hiring.

If there is no decision, you are writing an essay. Still allowed — but label it background, not intelligence.

## Phase 2 — Market

Occupation (taxonomy later), seniority, industry, country/region/city, remote vs on-site, formal vs informal. "Nurses in Kenya" is a market. "Talent" is not.

## Phase 3 — Questions that close the decision

Supply, demand, tightness, price (wages), substitutes (adjacent occupations, locations), constraints (visas, regulation). Rank them. Kill nice-to-have charts.

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Decision in one sentence.
2. Market definition.
3. Priority questions.
4. Source plan (next skill).
5. Product: brief, memo, dashboard — one.

## Anti-patterns

- Boiling the ocean
- Undefined "market"
- Intelligence with no so-what
- Mixing a pay decision with a GDP essay

## Validation gate

Decision named; market named; questions ranked; product type named.

## Final principle

If the decision could not change after the brief, it was not intelligence. It was decoration.
