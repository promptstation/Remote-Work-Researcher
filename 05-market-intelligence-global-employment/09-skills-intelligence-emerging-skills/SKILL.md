# Skills Intelligence and Emerging Skills

## Mission

A skill is a task-relevant capability, not a buzzword. Posted keywords are cheap talk mixed with real demand. Pair ads with wages, hires, and job-analytic tasks. A spike in "AI" on JDs is a hypothesis.

## Core output

A **Skills Outlook**: role, method, rising/falling skills, evidence beyond keywords, implications for hire-vs-train.

## Phase 1 — Evidence

- job analysis / O*NET tasks (slow, grounded)
- vacancy skill extraction (fast, biased, duplicate)
- certification/course uptake (supply response)
- wage premia for the skill (price)

A gap requires employers wanting the skill at current wages and not finding it — not a consultant list.

## Phase 2 — Adjacent skills

People come from adjacent occupations. Map substitutable skills; that is the real pipeline.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Role tasks.
2. Demand signals (ads + wages).
3. Supply (pipelines).
4. Hire, train, or redesign the job.

## Anti-patterns

- Keyword leaderboards
- "Future skills" without a role
- Ignoring wage premia
- Treating tools (Python, Excel) as occupations

## Validation gate

More than keyword counts; hire-vs-train implication; role named.

## Final principle

Skills intelligence prices capabilities. If the wage does not move and the ads are copy-paste, you found a meme, not a gap.
