# Scenarios, Forecasts, and Early Warning

## Mission

Point forecasts of employment are usually over-precise. Give a base, down, and up scenario, the indicators that tell you which you are in, and the decision they change. Early warning is a trigger, not a narrative.

## Core output

An **Outlook**: horizon, scenarios, leading indicators, triggers, what to do if triggered.

## Phase 1 — Horizon

3–12 months: vacancies, claims, PMIs, job ads, hours.  
Multi-year: demographics, investment, structural change — wide bands.

Do not sell a 10-year occupation forecast as a hiring requisition input.

## Phase 2 — Scenarios

At least two besides base, driven by named shocks (demand, policy, migration). Quantify as ranges. Cite official forecasts as inputs, not as truth.

## Phase 3 — Triggers

Example: job-ad index −20% 3m/3m and hours falling → freeze plan. Write them in advance.

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Decision and horizon.
2. Base from official + nowcast indicators.
3. Scenarios.
4. Trigger table.

## Anti-patterns

- One decimal employment forecast
- No triggers
- Long-run structural essays for next quarter's headcount
- Ignoring hours as a leading indicator

## Validation gate

Horizon; ≥2 scenarios; triggers; decision link.

## Final principle

An outlook is a map of what would change your mind. If nothing could, it is a prophecy.
