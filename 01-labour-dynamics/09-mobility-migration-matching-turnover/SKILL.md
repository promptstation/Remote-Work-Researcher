# Mobility, Migration, Matching, and Job Turnover

## Mission

Treat mobility as selection plus matching, not as a volume of bodies. Who moves, from where, into which jobs, and who is left behind determine wage and employment effects. A migration or turnover headline without selection is not an analysis.

## When this skill governs

Use for internal and international migration, commuting, job-to-job moves, quits vs layoffs, tenure, matching quality, brain drain/gain, and the labour-market effects of immigration.

## Core output

Deliver a **Mobility Note**:

1. Type of move (international, regional, firm-to-firm, occupation)
2. Selection (who moves on observables and likely unobservables)
3. Receiving-market definition
4. Short-run vs long-run adjustment
5. Wage and employment effects by competing group
6. Turnover composition (quits, layoffs, job-to-job)
7. Caveats on displacement vs complementarity

## Phase 1 — Name the move

Objects that are not interchangeable:

- international migration
- rural–urban and regional migration
- commuting
- occupation or industry switch
- employer switch (job-to-job)
- unemployment-to-job and job-to-unemployment

Each has a different cost, selection rule, and receiving market.

## Phase 2 — Selection (Roy / Borjas)

People move when expected gain exceeds cost. Selection on skills depends on relative inequality and returns:

- if the destination pays skill more, movers tend to be positively selected
- if the origin pays skill more, movers may be negatively selected on skill
- networks and policy (points systems, family reunification, refugees) override pure Roy selection

Never treat "immigrants" as a homogeneous labour type. Split by education, occupation, legal status, and years since arrival.

Return and circular migration select again. Stocks are not inflows.

## Phase 3 — Receiving-market effects

Immigration (or in-migration) is a labour-supply shock to specific markets, not to "the economy."

Spatial approach: compare local labour markets with different inflows. Problem: natives out-migrate, and immigrants choose booming places.

National skill-cell approach: compare education-experience cells. Problem: cell definitions and within-cell substitution.

Task/occupation approach: immigrants and natives may specialise in different tasks (language-intensive vs manual), producing complementarity.

Professional rule:

- define the competing group
- allow native mobility and task specialisation
- separate short-run (capital and natives fixed) from long-run (capital adjusts, natives reallocate)
- do not quote a single "immigration wage effect"

Public-finance and fiscal incidence are not the same as wage effects. Keep them separate.

## Phase 4 — Job matching and turnover

A match has quality. Separation can be efficient (bad match, better outside option) or inefficient (credit constraints, UI design, last-in-first-out).

Flows:

- **Quits** usually procyclical (better outside options)
- **Layoffs** countercyclical
- **Job-to-job** moves are a wage-ladder channel (on-the-job search)
- Tenure raises wages in part via specific capital and in part via selection (survivors are better matches)

Do not treat all turnover as bad. Excess churn has training costs; too little churn freezes misallocation.

JOLTS-type data: hires, quits, layoffs, vacancies. Use them with unemployment to read tightness (see unemployment/search skill for Beveridge). Here, use them to describe reallocation.

## Phase 5 — Internal mobility and spatial equilibrium

Regional wage gaps persist because of amenities, housing, moving costs, and information. Spatial equilibrium: higher wages may capitalise into rents, not utility.

Rural–urban migration (Harris–Todaro and later): expected urban wage, informal urban jobs, and unemployment can coexist with continued inflows. Do not treat urban unemployment as proof that migration was a mistake.

## Phase 6 — Research workflow

1. Name the move and the market.
2. Characterise selection.
3. Identify the competing native or incumbent group.
4. Choose short vs long run.
5. Separate complementarity from substitution.
6. For turnover questions, split quits, layoffs, and job-to-job, and say whether churn is cyclical or structural.

## Anti-patterns

- Homogeneous "immigrants" labour supply shock
- Using a local correlation of inflows and wages as causal
- Ignoring native out-migration and capital adjustment
- Treating all quits as dysfunction
- Equating tenure-wage slopes with pure productivity growth
- Mixing stocks and flows of migrants
- Spatial wage gaps as pure productivity gaps (ignoring housing)

## Decision heuristic

1. What moved — people across borders, regions, or firms?
2. Who selected into the move?
3. Who competes with them?
4. Short run or long run?
5. Is this a wage-ladder story or a displacement story?

## Validation gate

- Type of mobility named
- Selection addressed
- Receiving market defined
- Horizon stated
- Competing group named
- Quits vs layoffs split when turnover is the question

## Final principle

Mobility reallocates selected people into selected matches. Volume without selection is not labour dynamics.
