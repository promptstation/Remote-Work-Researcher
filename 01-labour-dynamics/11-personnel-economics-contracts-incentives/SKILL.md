# Personnel Economics, Contracts, and Incentive Pay

## Mission

Inside the firm, the labour contract is incomplete: effort, care, and cooperation are not fully contractible. Pay systems are technologies for eliciting effort without creating the wrong effort. Diagnose the contract, the measurable performance signal, and the distortion before recommending a bonus.

## When this skill governs

Use for incentive pay, commissions, piece rates, profit sharing, tournaments, promotion rules, efficiency wages, executive compensation, monitoring, and internal labour markets.

## Core output

Deliver an **Incentive System Note**:

1. What behaviour the firm actually wants
2. What is measurable (the signal)
3. Current pay contract (fixed, piece, bonus, tournament, career)
4. Predicted effort, sorting, and distortions
5. Monitoring and gaming
6. Redesign that matches the signal quality
7. What not to incentivise

## Phase 1 — Incomplete contracts

The firm wants effort, quality, cooperation, and care of long-term assets. The contract can usually specify hours, output counts, and some KPIs — not true effort.

If the signal is a noisy or partial measure of the goal, strong incentives will distort (multitasking, Holmström–Milgrom). Weak incentives plus monitoring or selection may dominate.

Default: **do not attach high-powered incentives to a bad measure.**

## Phase 2 — The menu of instruments

- **Fixed wage + monitoring:** works when output is hard to measure and monitoring is cheap
- **Piece rates / commissions:** high effort on the measured unit; risk on the worker; quality and cooperation suffer if unmeasured
- **Bonuses on KPIs:** only as good as the KPI
- **Tournaments / relative pay:** useful when common shocks dominate; collusion and sabotage risk
- **Promotions (career concerns):** incentives without current bonus cost; limited slots; influence activity
- **Profit sharing / ESOP:** weak individual incentive, better for cooperation; free-rider problem in large teams
- **Efficiency wages:** pay above market to raise cost of job loss, reduce shirking, cut turnover, or gift-exchange

Pick the instrument that matches signal quality and team production, not the one that is fashionable.

## Phase 3 — Risk, sorting, and participation

Incentives shift both effort and who applies.

- risk-averse workers demand a premium to bear bonus risk
- piece rates attract high-output types (Lazear sorting) — a measured productivity jump may be selection, not effort
- clawbacks and deferrals change the risk timing (relevant for finance and executive pay)

Always split **incentive effect** vs **sorting effect** when a pay plan "worked."

## Phase 4 — Efficiency wages and internal labour markets

Efficiency wage: \(w\) above reservation to buy effort or loyalty. Predicts involuntary unemployment in the market-level version; inside the firm, predicts wage compression at the bottom, monitoring intensity, and unemployment as discipline.

Internal labour markets: ports of entry, promotion ladders, shielded wages. They provide incentives and insurance. They also create insider rents and can misallocate if the ladder is the only incentive.

Do not treat the internal wage structure as a market wage. It is an incentive and insurance system.

## Phase 5 — Gaming and multitasking

If you pay on a metric, you get the metric.

Typical failures:

- sales commissions that destroy service
- call-centre AHT targets that destroy resolution
- publication or citation bonuses that destroy research quality
- forced ranking that destroys cooperation
- executive EPS bonuses that buy back shares

Before installing a KPI bonus, write down the easiest way to raise the KPI without raising the goal. If that way is attractive, do not use the bonus, or add a hard quality gate.

## Phase 6 — Executive and team pay

Executive pay: principal–agent with weak monitoring, outside options, and governance failure. High pay can be incentive, talent price, or rent. Do not assume any one.

Teams: individual piece rates undermine help; group bonuses free-ride. Mixed systems, peer monitoring, and small groups are the usual repair.

## Phase 7 — Research workflow

1. Write the true objective in one sentence.
2. List available signals and their corruption modes.
3. Map the current contract to predicted behaviour.
4. Separate sorting from effort in any before/after.
5. Redesign: weaker power, better signal, quality gates, or selection/monitoring instead of pay.

## Anti-patterns

- High-powered incentives on a noisy or partial KPI
- Ignoring sorting when celebrating a productivity jump
- Treating internal wages as spot-market wages
- Copying sales commissions into collaborative professional work
- "Pay for performance" as a slogan without a measure
- Ignoring risk-bearing costs
- Forced ranking in highly interdependent teams

## Decision heuristic

1. Can we measure the thing we want?
2. How will people game it?
3. Individual, relative, or career incentive?
4. Sorting or effort?
5. Is monitoring cheaper than a bonus?

## Validation gate

- Objective stated
- Signal quality assessed
- Distortion path named
- Sorting vs effort split if evaluating a plan
- No KPI bonus recommended on an easily gamed metric without a gate

## Final principle

Incentives work. That is the danger. Pay only for a signal that survives the easiest game.
