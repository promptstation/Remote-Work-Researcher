# Informal Work, Structural Change, and Developing-Country Labour Dynamics

## Mission

Do not analyse a developing labour market as a missing-data version of a US wage sector. Informality, dualism, unpaid family work, and agriculture are the market. Unemployment can be low while working poverty is high. Choose models and indicators that allow that.

## When this skill governs

Use for informal employment, informal sector, rural labour, agrarian relations, rural–urban migration, working poverty, structural transformation, and any labour question set in a market where wage employment is not the modal job.

## Core output

Deliver a **Developing Labour-Market Note**:

1. Structure of employment (agriculture / industry / services; wage / self-employed / contributing family)
2. Informal employment vs informal sector
3. Labour underutilization beyond LU1 (hours, LU4, working poverty)
4. Rural and urban dualism
5. Binding constraint (demand for formal jobs, skills, regulation, capital, seasonality)
6. What a rich-country indicator would misread here

## Phase 1 — Indicators that do not lie in this setting

LU1 is often a weak welfare statistic where the poor cannot afford to search without working.

Prefer this bundle:

- EPR and LFPR by sex and rural/urban
- status in employment (ICSE)
- informal employment share (job-based) and informal sector share (enterprise-based)
- time-related underemployment and LU4
- working poverty (employed below a poverty line)
- hours and seasonality
- sector shares (agriculture vs industry vs services)

If poverty among workers is high and LU1 is low, the problem is job quality and productivity, not "full employment."

## Phase 2 — Informality, defined

**Informal sector:** unincorporated enterprises without formal accounts / registration (enterprise-based).

**Informal employment:** jobs not covered in law or practice by formal arrangements (job-based). Includes informal jobs in formal firms.

**Unregistered work** is not automatically informal employment under ILO rules; follow the operational definition used in the source.

Never use "informal sector" as a synonym for "all bad jobs." Formal jobs can be poor; some informal work is viable self-employment.

Measurement depends on LFS modules. Payroll data will miss the object.

## Phase 3 — Dualism and models

Lewis dualism: a traditional sector with surplus labour and a modern wage sector. The wage in the modern sector can stay flat while labour is pulled out of agriculture, until the turning point.

Harris–Todaro: expected urban wage, urban unemployment or informal queues, and continued migration.

Use these as organising frames, then check:

- is rural labour actually surplus, or seasonal and gendered?
- is the urban informal sector a queue for formal jobs or a distinct productive sector?
- are wages in the modern sector set by institutions (minimum wages, public pay) rather than by surplus labour?

Do not assume surplus labour. Do not assume the informal sector is only a waiting room.

## Phase 4 — Rural labour

Agrarian labour mixes own-account farming, casual wage labour, attached labour, and non-farm rural work. Contracts (daily, seasonal, share) are the market.

Women’s work is systematically undercounted if own-use production and contributing family work are poorly probed (19th ICLS matters).

Seasonality: annual unemployment can look low while lean-season hunger is the labour-market fact. Use quarters or agricultural calendars.

## Phase 5 — Structural transformation

Development reallocates labour from agriculture to industry and then services. Watch:

- productivity gaps between sectors
- whether people leave agriculture into formal industry or into urban informal services
- premature deindustrialisation (services absorbing labour without industrial wage jobs)

A falling agricultural employment share is not success if urban absorption is informal and poor.

## Phase 6 — Policy that fits dualism

- Minimum wages and EPL bite the formal island and can widen dualism if enforcement is partial
- Social protection often misses informal workers unless designed for them (not only UI for employees)
- Formalisation policy: distinguish tax registration, social insurance, and job-quality upgrading — they are not one lever
- Public jobs and rural employment guarantees are labour-market institutions, not only welfare

Do not recommend UI-as-in-OECD as the first instrument for a 70% informal market.

## Phase 7 — Research workflow

1. Employment composition table (sector × status × sex × rural/urban).
2. Informality and working poverty next to LU1.
3. Choose dualism/migration frame or reject it with evidence.
4. Name the binding constraint on better jobs.
5. Evaluate any proposed law on the informal margin, not only the formal wage.

## Anti-patterns

- Using LU1 as the development labour-market score
- Treating informality as illegal residual
- Importing competitive wage-employment models without a formal sector large enough to carry them
- Ignoring unpaid family and own-use production
- Annual averages that hide agricultural seasons
- Formalisation slogans without insurance or demand
- Equating self-employment with entrepreneurship

## Decision heuristic

1. What is the modal job?
2. Is the problem joblessness or poor work?
3. Informal employment or informal sector?
4. Rural seasonality or urban dualism?
5. Who is outside the law you are evaluating?

## Validation gate

- Status and sector composition shown
- Informal employment defined correctly
- LU1 not used alone
- Sex and rural/urban splits present
- Policy assessed on the informal margin

## Final principle

In a dual labour market the headline unemployment rate can be a vanity metric. Count how people work, under what contract, and at what productivity — then speak.
