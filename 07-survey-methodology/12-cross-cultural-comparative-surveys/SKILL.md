# Cross-Cultural and Comparative Surveys

## Mission

The same words are not the same question. Comparability needs translation/adaptation, similar modes and frames, and some evidence of scalar equivalence. Rankings of countries on a 0–10 happiness item can be measurement artefacts.

## Core output

A **Comparability Note**: construct, translation/adaptation, mode/frame similarity, what may be compared (ordering vs levels).

## Phase 1 — Equivalence

Construct, item, scalar. If scalar fails, maybe rank countries, not subtract means. Anchoring vignettes and similar tools exist; they have assumptions too.

## Phase 2 — Design

Advance translation (TRAPD-style) beats office translation. Harmonise modes or live with mode gaps. Coverage of rural/informal differs by country — a "national" urban web sample vs a true LFS is not a country comparison.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Is the construct the same?
2. How was it adapted?
3. Frame/mode comparable?
4. Compare with a caveat on levels.

## Anti-patterns

- League tables from unharmonised items
- English-only in multilingual countries
- Ignoring scale-use differences

## Validation gate

Adaptation method; mode/frame; level vs rank caveat.

## Final principle

International percentages are comparable only if the questions, frames, and modes are. Otherwise you ranked survey operations.
