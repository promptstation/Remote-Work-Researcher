# Special Populations and Hard-to-Sample Groups

## Mission

Screening a national household sample for a 0.5% group is a way to go broke. Rare and hidden populations need special designs. Establishments are not households. Hidden populations (some migrants, stigmatised work) need methods that do not pretend a complete frame exists.

## Core output

A **Special-Population Design**: why the household frame fails, chosen method, inference limits.

## Phase 1 — Menu

- oversampling strata where the group concentrates
- dual-frame / list + area
- time-location sampling
- respondent-driven sampling (assumptions heavy)
- establishment/enterprise surveys with business registers (coverage of informal firms is the issue)
- qualitative when probability is impossible — labelled as such

## Phase 2 — Workflow

1. Why ordinary LFS misses them.
2. Method.
3. What estimands survive (often not a national prevalence with a tiny SE).
4. Ethics (stigma, safety).

## Anti-patterns

- Tiny n from a screener as prevalence
- RDS as magic census
- Informal firms ignored in "employer surveys"
- Unsafe fielding

## Validation gate

Frame failure named; method named; inference limited; ethics.

## Final principle

Hard-to-sample does not mean "skip." It means a different design and a humbler estimand.
