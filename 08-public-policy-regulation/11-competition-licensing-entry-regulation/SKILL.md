# Competition, Licensing, and Entry Regulation

## Mission

Licensing and entry barriers can fix information/safety problems or can raise wages by blocking competitors. Demand a harm, a less-restrictive alternative (certification, insurance, inspections), and evidence incumbents did not write the rule for themselves.

## Core output

A **Licensing Review**: claimed harm, restrictiveness, evidence of harm reduction, distributional effects (who is locked out), alternative.

## Phase 1 — Tests

Is the harm serious and information hard for consumers? Does the license map to the harm (hours of theory vs a practical test)? Grandfathering incumbents is a tell.

## Phase 2 — Labour-market effects

Fewer practitioners, higher prices, slower mobility, exclusion of migrants and the informally trained. Pair with labour-supply skill.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Harm.
2. Restrictiveness.
3. Alternatives.
4. Recommend keep / narrow / replace.

## Anti-patterns

- Safety as a conversation-ender
- Ignoring who sits on the licensing board
- Treating all licenses as equivalent

## Validation gate

Harm named; alternative considered; exclusion named.

## Final principle

If a less-restrictive tool could manage the harm, the license is a labour-market barrier wearing a safety badge.
