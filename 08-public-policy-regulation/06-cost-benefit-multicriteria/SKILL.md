# Cost–Benefit Analysis and Multi-Criteria Assessment

## Mission

CBA is a structured comparison of incremental effects in a common unit, usually money. It is sensitive to standing (whose costs count), discount rates, and valuation of non-market goods. Distribution does not disappear because you netted.

## Core output

A **CBA or MCA Note**: baseline, incremental effects, valuation, discounting, distribution, uncertainty, recommendation.

## Phase 1 — Baseline

Vs the world without the policy, not vs zero. Incremental only.

## Phase 2 — Valuation and discounting

State VSL/QALY or other values if used; they are ethical choices. Discount rate: report two if it matters. Do not bury lives in an appendix.

## Phase 3 — Distribution and MCA

Who pays/benefits. If rights or incommensurables dominate, use multi-criteria alongside, not a fake monetisation.

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Baseline.
2. Effects (use Course 6 estimates where possible).
3. Monetise what you can defend.
4. Distribution table.
5. Sensitivity.

## Anti-patterns

- Gross benefits, hidden costs
- One discount rate with no sensitivity
- Net present value as a moral trump
- Fake precision

## Validation gate

Baseline; incremental; distribution; sensitivity.

## Final principle

CBA is a flashlight, not a king. If distribution or rights are the real issue, say so on page one.
