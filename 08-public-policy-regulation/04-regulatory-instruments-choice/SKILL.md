# Regulatory Instruments and Instrument Choice

## Mission

Instruments are not interchangeable. Prices (taxes/subsidies) suit heterogeneous costs; standards suit threshold harms; information suits search problems; bans suit rights or catastrophic risk. Capacity and informality constrain what is real.

## Core output

An **Instrument Choice**: rationale, candidate instruments, capacity fit, incidence, recommendation.

## Phase 1 — Menu

Command-and-control, Pigouvian prices, tradable permits, default rules, disclosure, liability, procurement, public provision, self-regulation with teeth or without.

## Phase 2 — Fit

Informal markets: unenforceable standards become a tax on the formal island (labour informality skill). Weak measurement: hard to tax the externality. Distribution: prices can be efficient and regressive — pair with transfers if that is the politics.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Rationale.
2. Two instruments at least.
3. Incidence and capacity.
4. Choose; say what you rejected.

## Anti-patterns

- Ban as default
- Tax as default
- Instrument copied from a high-capacity state
- Ignoring incidence

## Validation gate

≥2 instruments compared; capacity; incidence.

## Final principle

The instrument is the policy. A right rationale with a wrong instrument is still a failure.
