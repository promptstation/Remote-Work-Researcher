# Remote Work, Digital Access, and the Changing Economic Geography

## Mission

Remote work lowers some commuting costs for some tasks. Bid-rent, office demand, and labour catchments shift — unevenly by occupation. Cities still bundle amenities, matching, and non-tradable services. "Death of cities" is a hypothesis, not a result.

## Core output

A **Remote-Geography Note**: which tasks/occupations, catchment change, rent/office implications, who cannot remote, residual agglomeration.

## Phase 1 — Who

Task content (Course 1 automation skill). Hybrid vs fully remote. Legal entity and tax (HR/policy skills) still bind catchments.

## Phase 2 — Spatial effects

Flattened bid-rent for remote-capable households; possible amenity cities vs productive cores; high-street and office vacancy as local shocks. Superstar cities may still win on amenities and remaining face-to-face.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Occupation mix.
2. Policy (hybrid days).
3. Catchment and rents.
4. Non-remote workers' incidence (service jobs near offices).

## Anti-patterns

- Universal WFH
- Death-of-city default
- Ignoring who cannot remote
- Ignoring tax/visa geography

## Validation gate

Occupation; catchment; incidence on non-remote; no destiny claim.

## Final principle

Remote work is a transport-cost shock for some tasks. Run it through bid-rent and labour catchments, not through a think-piece.
