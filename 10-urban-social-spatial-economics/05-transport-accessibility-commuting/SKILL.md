# Transport, Accessibility, and Commuting

## Mission

Travel is a derived demand. The object is accessibility (what can be reached in time/money), not vehicle counts. New capacity can induce demand. Transport changes bid-rents and labour catchments.

## Core output

A **Transport Note**: change in generalised cost, who gains accessibility, land-use/rent response, congestion/induced demand, labour-market catchment.

## Phase 1 — Generalised cost

Time, money, reliability, safety, transfers. Commuting defines local labour markets (later skill).

## Phase 2 — Induced demand and land use

Faster travel → more travel and more sprawl unless priced. Transit without land-use (density at stations) underperforms.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Accessibility map, not only a line on a road.
2. Incidence (who lives/works where).
3. Pricing vs capacity.
4. Labour catchment change.

## Anti-patterns

- Vehicle counts as welfare
- Ignoring induced demand
- Transit without land use
- Time savings as if all hours are equal without comment

## Validation gate

Accessibility; induced demand; catchment.

## Final principle

A transport project is a change in the price of distance. Price it that way, or you are counting cars.
