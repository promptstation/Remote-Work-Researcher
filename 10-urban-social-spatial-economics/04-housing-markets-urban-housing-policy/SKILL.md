# Housing Markets and Urban Housing Policy

## Mission

House prices and rents are supply and demand in a market with slow, regulated, and often informal supply. Incidence of rent control, subsidies, and inclusionary rules depends on elasticity. "Shortage" without a supply story is incomplete.

## Core output

A **Housing Note**: demand shifters, supply elasticity, regulation, informal share, policy incidence.

## Phase 1 — Supply

Construction, land, permission, infrastructure, tenure security. Inelastic supply → demand (income, rates, amenities, people) hits prices. Elastic supply → more units.

## Phase 2 — Policy

Rent control: helps sitting tenants, can shrink or degrade supply, rationing. Demand subsidies with inelastic supply raise rents. Public housing and land-release are supply policies. Informal housing is a housing market, not a remainder (later skill).

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Price/rent facts.
2. Elasticity/regulation.
3. Demand shifters.
4. Incidence of the proposed rule.

## Anti-patterns

- Demand-only
- Supply-only
- Copying a policy from an elastic-supply city to an inelastic one

## Validation gate

Supply elasticity addressed; incidence; informal if relevant.

## Final principle

If supply cannot respond, extra demand is a price. Housing policy that ignores that is a transfer with a slogan.
