# Informal Urbanism, Slums, and Cities in Developing Countries

## Mission

Many cities grow through informal land and labour. Tenure insecurity, self-built housing, and dual labour markets are the structure. Bid-rent still helps, but "slum" is not non-economic space.

## Core output

A **Developing-City Note**: formal vs informal land and work, tenure, services, commuting, policy that does not assume titles and wage jobs.

## Phase 1 — Land

Informal occupation, incremental building, limited collateral. Regularisation vs eviction vs servicing. Supply of accessible land, not only CBD models.

## Phase 2 — Work

Urban informal employment, home-based work, long commutes from peripheral settlements. Pair with Course 1 informality skill.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Share informal in housing and work.
2. Accessibility of the poor.
3. Service gaps.
4. Policy on tenure/services/transport, not only CBD beautification.

## Anti-patterns

- US monocentric photocopy
- Slum as residual
- Ignoring tenure
- Formal job stats as the urban labour market

## Validation gate

Informal land and labour both addressed; tenure; accessibility of the poor.

## Final principle

If the modal house and job are informal, a formal-only urban model is a downtown theory. Widen the map.
