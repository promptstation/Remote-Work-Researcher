# Sorting, Amenities, and Neighbourhood Change

## Mission

People sort on amenities, schools, and housing prices. Neighbourhood change is prices, in-movers, out-movers, and incumbents. Gentrification is not one number; it is a set of incidence questions.

## Core output

A **Sorting Note**: amenity shock, price response, who moves in/out, incumbent tenants vs owners.

## Phase 1 — Tiebout-like

Local public goods and amenities attract those who value them and can pay. Capitalisation into rents/house prices.

## Phase 2 — Change

Upgrade amenities or access → demand up → rents up if supply fixed → tenants lose, owners gain, composition changes. Measure displacement carefully (who left vs who would have left anyway).

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Shock.
2. Prices.
3. Flows.
4. Incidence by tenure.

## Anti-patterns

- Gentrification as a moral primitive without prices
- Ignoring owners vs tenants
- Correlation of amenities with outcomes as sorting-proof

## Validation gate

Prices; movers; tenure incidence.

## Final principle

Neighbourhoods change when the bid for the place changes. Follow prices and movers, not only the café count.
