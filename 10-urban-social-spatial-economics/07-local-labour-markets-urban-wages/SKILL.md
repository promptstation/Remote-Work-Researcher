# Local Labour Markets and Urban Wages

## Mission

A labour market has a geography: commuting basins, not always districts. Thick markets match better and may pay more (agglomeration). Local shocks are absorbed by wages, unemployment, participation, and migration — with lags.

## Core output

A **Local LM Note**: market definition, shock, adjustment margins, urban wage premium composition vs agglomeration.

## Phase 1 — Define the market

Commuting flows, travel time. Administrative units can split a market or glue two.

## Phase 2 — Adjustment

Bartik-type shocks, plant closures: out-migration, non-participation, wage drops. Informal urban work absorbs in developing cities.

## Phase 3 — Urban wage premium

Part composition (skills sort into cities), part true agglomeration. Do not call the raw city dummy a productivity miracle.

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Basin.
2. Shock.
3. Margins.
4. Composition vs agglomeration for wage gaps.

## Anti-patterns

- District = market
- Ignoring migration as adjustment
- Raw urban wage as agglomeration

## Validation gate

Market geographically defined; adjustment margins; composition.

## Final principle

Labour markets are catchments. Draw the commute, then apply labour economics.
