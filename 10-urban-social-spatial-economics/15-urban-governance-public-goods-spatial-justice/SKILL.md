# Urban Governance, Public Goods, and Spatial Justice

## Mission

Cities are governments as well as markets. Local public goods (schools, safety, water, streets) are produced and rationed in space. Fiscal capacity, jurisdictions, and politics shape who gets them. Spatial inequality is partly a public-finance map.

## Core output

A **Spatial-Governance Note**: which public good, which jurisdiction, finance, who is underserved, instrument.

## Phase 1 — Goods

Local vs metropolitan vs national. Spillovers (education, congestion) argue for higher-level finance. Fragmented municipalities can sort and exclude (Tiebout with teeth).

## Phase 2 — Justice

Access by neighbourhood, informal settlements without services, commuting poor. Rights and distribution (Course 8) apply. Do not treat underservice as revealed preference.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Good.
2. Who provides and who pays.
3. Map of access.
4. Instrument (finance, jurisdiction, targeting).

## Anti-patterns

- Market-only urbanism
- Ignoring metropolitan spillovers
- Informal areas as outside the polity
- One rich municipality as a model for the poor neighbour without fiscal incidence

## Validation gate

Jurisdiction; finance; access map; distribution.

## Final principle

Space is allocated by prices and by governments. If you omit the second, you will misread the map as only a market.
