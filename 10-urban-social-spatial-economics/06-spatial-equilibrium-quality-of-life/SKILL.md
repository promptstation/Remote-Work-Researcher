# Spatial Equilibrium and Quality of Life

## Mission

In spatial equilibrium, people are indifferent across places after wages, rents, and amenities. High wages may pay for high rents or disamenities, not only for higher productivity. Moving costs and housing supply slow equalisation.

## Core output

A **Spatial-Eq Note**: wage gap, rent gap, amenity list, productivity vs amenity split, mobility frictions.

## Phase 1 — Accounting

Real wage ≈ nominal − housing − commute ± amenities. Do not rank cities on nominal wages.

## Phase 2 — Limits

Not everyone is mobile (poor, tied, informal). Housing inelasticity capitalises amenity into owners, not tenants. Use as a benchmark.

## Phase 3 — Workflow

1. Nominal wages and rents.
2. Amenities/disamenities.
3. Who is mobile.
4. Interpretation of the residual.

## Anti-patterns

- Nominal wage league tables
- Ignoring housing
- Assuming perfect mobility

## Validation gate

Rents and amenities in the wage comparison; mobility caveat.

## Final principle

A wage is a local price. Subtract the local cost of living in space, or do not call it a better job.
