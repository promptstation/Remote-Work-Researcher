# Heterogeneity, Mechanisms, and External Validity

## Mission

Subgroup effects are easy to invent. Mechanisms are extra assumptions. External validity is a new identification problem. A LATE in one site is not a national policy parameter.

## Core output

A **Transport Note**: original estimand/population, subgroup plan, mechanism tests, what would have to be true to apply elsewhere.

## Phase 1 — Heterogeneity

Pre-specify a few slices. Otherwise FDR/exploratory labels. Interactions need enough n in each cell. Difference in effects ≠ "works only for."

## Phase 2 — Mechanisms

Mediation needs extra assumptions (no unconfounded mediator). Multiple outcomes along a chain are suggestive, not proof. Do not claim mechanism from a coefficient changing when you add a post-treatment variable without comment.

## Phase 3 — External validity

Population, site, scale, equilibrium (partial vs general), compliance. Scaling a small NGO RCT to a ministry programme often fails on those.

## Phase 4 — Workflow

1. Who was the study on?
2. Pre-specified slices vs exploratory.
3. Mechanism as hypothesis.
4. Transport sentence for the decision population.

## Anti-patterns

- 20 slices, one winner
- Mediator-in-regression as mechanism proof
- "This RCT shows the policy works" in a new country with no argument

## Validation gate

Population named; slices labelled; transport not automatic.

## Final principle

Effects are local objects. Moving them is extra work, not a courtesy of the abstract.
